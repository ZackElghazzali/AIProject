# Demo video

Two minutes. Upload to D2L with your submission, Fri Nov 20.

Two points of your final grade. Partly so you practise showing your work to
people who did not build it, and partly because in three weeks you will want
the recording more than you want the code.

## What to record

Screen capture with your voice over it. No editing required and no production
value is graded, a single take with a stumble in it scores the same as a
polished one.

Cover, in this order:

1. **Run it.** Show the app working on a provided image. This is the part
   people came for; do it first, not last.
2. **Show one thing under the hood.** One function, one design decision, one
   thing that was harder than it looked. Not a tour of the repo.
3. **Show it failing.** Run it on an image from the `far` group and let the
   miss happen on camera. Say what it means.
4. **Say who did what.** Fifteen seconds. Every name.

## Rules

- Two minutes is a limit, not a target. Ninety seconds is fine.
- Every team member speaks at least once.
- No faces on camera except your own, and only if you want to be on camera.
  Nothing in this project requires filming a person.
- Say which mode you built (`blur`, `count` or `overlay`) somewhere in the
  first thirty seconds.

## Rubric: 2 points

| | Points |
|---|---:|
| The app is shown working, on screen, on a real input | 0.75 |
| One technical point explained clearly rather than narrated | 0.5 |
| A failure case shown and interpreted | 0.5 |
| Every member speaks; contributions named | 0.25 |

Over two minutes loses 0.25. A video that shows only slides and no running
software tops out at 1.0, however good the slides.

### Why the failure case is worth as much as the explanation

Most demo videos are advertisements. This one is graded partly on showing the
thing not working, because you will spend a career being asked whether
something is ready, and the useful answer always includes where it breaks. A
team that shows the `far` miss and says what it implies has done the harder and
more valuable thing.

## Recording it

Anything that captures a screen and a microphone works. Zoom recording to your
own machine is the path of least resistance if you have nothing else: start a
meeting alone, share your screen, record locally.

Upload the file to D2L, or upload elsewhere and submit a link that works
without a login: check it in a private window before you submit.
